# The life challenge is a food factory, not Neo Vitae

Life's Vital Essence is made from food, not blood. A team runs a factory that extracts raw crops and animals (Agritech Evolved, Animal Pen) and turns them into ingredients (Pantry for Blockheads, furnaces) and then into meals (Farmer's Delight's Cooking Pot). It keeps the meals from rotting in Cooking for Blockheads' fridges and infuses the Vital Essence on a Craftworks Assembler from a set of distinct meals of at least a minimum quality (Quality Food). Neo Vitae leaves the pack entirely. Under it, life was played by bleeding into altars, not by building, and its last step stood on Neo Vitae's own blocks, not on a chain the team automates as the other three Transmuted Essences do. This overturns ADR 0011's rule that the pool is vanilla's foods and never a food mod: food mods come in where they serve the factory. The handicap stays ADR 0011's: hunger drains faster, and the Diet is the only rule on variety, so Spice of Life: Carrot Edition goes.

We could have kept Neo Vitae and only automated its blood, but its rituals, the Demon Realm and Tau would still be what the challenge is about. A food factory on vanilla's foods alone has too few steps and too little variety for four tiers to grow upstream.

## Consequences

- Spoiled is always on, not only while life is played, and spoils at one rate. Only fridges keep food, so storage is part of the factory.
- The modded machines that make meals don't keep Quality Food's quality, so the companion mod carries it through them and rolls it at harvest.
- The chain's steps that a player would do by hand, the Cutting Board and butchering, are never locked. The companion mod's Homunculus can work them as chain steps.
- The seeds, saplings and animals that start the raw step come from the Shop, not the mixes. The Advanced mix holds life's chain machines, as it holds the other chains'.
- The Diet, faster hunger and the hand drive's food cost are unchanged. Neo Vitae's Demon Realm trip and its Ritual of Full Stomach go with it.
